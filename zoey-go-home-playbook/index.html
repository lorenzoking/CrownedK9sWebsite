Know your pup

Who Zoey is

Personality

  • Very smart - Zoey learns quickly and notices patterns in your home.
  • Values freedom - she does best when rules are clear and earned freedom is part of the reward system.
  • Food motivated - use meals, treats, and food-based rewards to keep training fair and fun.
  • Great temperament - she is a sweet, capable puppy with a bright future.

Energy and follow-through

Zoey has spurts of puppy energy and can be slightly stubborn. That means consistency and follow-through matter. If you ask for something, help her finish it calmly instead of repeating yourself until she tunes you out.

Helpful tool: she can be a little whiney when bored. A frozen Kong, lick mat, or high-value chew helps her settle and keeps her occupied.

Your daily structure

Potty rhythm, crate rhythm, and calm resets

The four potty triggers

When in doubt, take Zoey out. Puppies need predictable chances to get it right.

  1. After eating - usually within 5-15 minutes of a meal
  2. After drinking - plan a quick trip after water
  3. After waking up - nap ends, potty starts
  4. After playing - when play slows down, go out right away

Zoey's morning rhythm

She poops first thing in the morning. Plan for an immediate potty trip when she wakes up. She typically averages about 2-3 poops per day, so keep outings predictable rather than waiting for a signal.

Sample rhythm

First potty -> short training -> eat -> potty -> alone crate time -> potty -> play -> potty -> Place or calm time.

Alone crate time every day

Zoey needs alone crate time daily to build independence and help prevent separation anxiety. Keep departures boring and return low-key.

Hurry Up - her potty cue

At her potty spot, say Hurry Up once. Stay neutral until she goes, then mark Yes and pay big. Consistency builds speed outside.

Reminder: when she whines in the crate, first ask whether she needs potty, food, or rest. If she is simply bored, use enrichment instead of letting her out for attention only.
02

Communication

Yes, Good, Free, and corrections

Zoey is smart enough to test gray areas. Clean communication and follow-through keep her confident instead of pushy.

Yes

Use Yes to mark the exact moment she gets it right, then reward. Example: her bottom hits the floor on Sit -> Yes -> food, praise, touch, or access.

Good

Use Good to tell her to keep holding the behavior. This is useful for Place, Down, and calm greetings.

Free

Free is her release word. After Sit, Down, Place, Wait, or Inside, she should wait for Free before getting up, bolting through a door, or diving at the food bowl.

Ant ant

Ant ant is her correction/interrupter. Say it once with calm authority, then immediately guide the right choice: reset the doorway, return to Place, or ask for a known command.

Important: do not nag. One cue, one correction if needed, then guide the outcome.
03

How she was taught

Training concepts that keep Zoey successful

Zoey was not just taught words; she was taught a system. These concepts are the homework that keeps her training from fading once she is home.

The staircase

Train step by step. If Zoey struggles, make the rep easier: less distance, less duration, fewer distractions. Win small, then build back up.

The cliffhanger

Keep reps short (often 1-2 minutes). End on a success while she is still engaged—leave her wanting more.

Clear communication

Use Yes, Good, and Free daily. Avoid repeating commands. One cue, one outcome.

The scoreboard

A rep counts when you release her with Free. Self-releases mean the next rep should be shorter or easier.

Follow-through

If Zoey ignores a known behavior, do not nag. Reset, make the rep easier, and help her succeed.

Independence in the crate

Daily alone crate time teaches her that quiet rest is normal and safe, even when you leave the room.

Daily homework: 3-5 minute reps, several times per day. Practice name response, Sit/Down duration, Place, Wait at doors, Inside, Come, morning potty, and calm crate exits. End on a win and release with Free.
04

What she knows

Commands Zoey knows: foundation

Zoey knows these behaviors. Your job is to help her learn that your voice, timing, and house rules mean the same thing. Duration behaviors end with Free when you are finished holding the position.

Zoey (name)

Means

Look at me / check in.

Your practice

Say her name once. When she looks, mark with Yes and reward. Her name should mean attention, not background noise.

Sit

Means

Bottom on the floor; a polite default before food, doors, and greetings.

Your practice

Ask for Sit before attention and meals. If you are holding the sit, release with Free when you are done.

Down

Means

Lie down and settle her body.

Your practice

Use short reps first. Mark with Yes, build duration with Good, then release with Free.

Place

Means

Go to her bed/mat and stay there until Free.

Your practice

Use Place during meals, guests, TV time, and whenever she needs an off switch.

05

Home life cues

Commands Zoey knows: house and movement

Inside

Means

Go into her crate.

Your practice

Use it for naps, bedtime, and safe management. Release with Free when you are ready for her to come out calmly.

Wait

Means

Pause - do not move through the threshold or toward the food until Free.

Your practice

Use Wait at meals, exterior doors, gates, and car doors.

Hurry Up

Means

Potty outside - now.

Your practice

Say Hurry Up at her potty spot; stay boring until she goes, then Yes and a real payoff.

Come

Means

Move toward you and check in.

Your practice

Call Come when she is likely to succeed, then reward generously. Keep early reps short and low-distraction so recall stays reliable.

Go to sleep

Means

At night, relax again and settle back down.

Your practice

Keep it boring and consistent. Say Go to sleep, reduce attention, and give her the chance to settle.

06

House manners

Freedom, consistency, and household manners

Consistency beats repetition

If you ask for Sit, Place, or Wait, follow through calmly until Zoey completes the behavior or you reset the rep. Mixed follow-through teaches a smart dog to wait you out.

Use food wisely

Reward calm choices before excitement takes over. Meals, doorways, greetings, and crate time are perfect places to pay her for good decisions.

Enrichment for whining

If Zoey gets vocal when she wants attention, redirect to a frozen Kong, lick mat, or high-value chew before she rehearses whining as a strategy.

Welcome home

You are not on your own

Zoey has a strong start, and now the goal is transfer: same rules, same words, same calm structure in your home. Questions are expected, especially during the first week.

Your homework plan

  1. Communication: use Yes, Good, and Free every day. One cue, one outcome.
  2. Morning potty: first trip outside right when she wakes up. She usually poops first thing in the morning.
  3. Triggers: honor all four potty triggers and use Hurry Up consistently outside.
  4. Alone crate time: give Zoey planned alone crate time every day to build independence.
  5. Follow-through: if you ask for a behavior, help her finish it calmly instead of repeating yourself.
  6. Enrichment: keep frozen Kongs or high-value chews ready for whiny or bored moments.
  7. Short sessions: practice commands in happy 3-5 minute reps so she learns your voice without getting frustrated.
  8. Reach out when unsure; do not guess.
